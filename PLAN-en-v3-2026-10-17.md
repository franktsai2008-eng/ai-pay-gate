# Sigil

## 1. What it is
Connect it once and your AI can pay for things. Every payment is checked before it goes out and gets a green, yellow or red light with reasons. Every agent belongs to a verified owner. Every finished trade is rated automatically and feeds a credit score that is hard to fake.

## 2. Why it matters
- AI agents already pay for things. x402, the agent payment standard backed by Coinbase, Stripe, Google, AWS and Visa, has passed about 191 million transactions (October 2026).
- Agents are easy to trick. Freysa was talked out of about $47K with one message (November 2024, a public contest). aixbt sent out about $100K after an attacker slipped two fake replies into its queue (March 2025).
- Agents have no trustworthy track record. You can't tell who is behind the other agent or whether it delivers, and ratings are easy to fake.

## 3. Business model
**Users**
- People who let an AI buy data or book services.
- Small companies whose AI buys supplies or pays other AIs.
- Developers who want their agents to pay and get paid.

**How people use it**
- Verify once (see section 5).
- AI side: one line of MCP config. Works with Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor.
- Human side: a phone app to see spending, approve payments and stop everything.
- Autonomy levels, from most to least careful:
  - Ask me every time.
  - Pay small amounts automatically and ask me for large ones.
  - Pay approved payees fully automatically.
  - Red is always blocked, whatever the level.

**Revenue**
- A small fee per payment (main income).
- Score lookups: wallets, marketplaces and platforms pay to check an agent's score and verification level, like a credit bureau for AI agents.
- Team plan: manage many agents and export ledgers for accounting.

**Why we win**
- Data flywheel: more trades make the score more accurate, which brings more users and more trades.
- Others, like Coinbase Payments MCP, focus on moving the money. We focus on whether a payment is safe, who is behind the other side, and whether it can be trusted.

**Rollout**
1. Free safety gate for developers already using x402.
2. Launch score lookups once enough trades have gone through.
3. Phone app for small companies and individuals.

## 4. System components
| Component | Job |
|---|---|
| MCP server | Tools the AI calls: check_payment, pay, balance, history, complain |
| Safety gate | Runs the six checks and sets the light |
| Identity verification | Checks the owner through a verification provider and writes an on-chain badge |
| Wallet contract | The user's own wallet, with spending rules enforced on-chain (e.g. Safe Allowance Module) |
| Escrow contract | Holds the buyer's money until the delivery is accepted |
| Deposit contract | Holds each seller's deposit and repays victims if the seller cheats |
| Rating engine | Rates each finished trade and updates scores |
| Fund-flow analyzer | Reads on-chain data and computes flow features |
| Private receiving | ERC-5564 stealth addresses plus view keys |
| Agent identity | ERC-8004 agent ID, linked to the owner's verification badge |
| Owner app | Spending, lights, rules, approvals, stop button |

## 5. Verified identity (real-name system)
Every agent has a real, verified owner behind it. The public sees a verification badge, never a name.

| Level | How | What it allows |
|---|---|---|
| 0: Wallet only | Nothing | Buying up to a small daily limit. Can't list in our market |
| 1: Unique person | World ID or Coinbase Verified (proves one real person, shares no name) | Selling up to tier 1 |
| 2: Full ID | Government ID plus selfie through a verification provider (e.g. Persona, Stripe Identity) | All tiers |
| 3: Verified business | Company registration check | All tiers, team plan, higher agent cap |

Rules:
- One verified person can run up to 10 agents. One verified business can run up to 100.
- Agents with the same verified owner count as one party. Trades between them earn no points, and they can't rate each other.
- A ban follows the person, not the wallet. A banned owner can't come back with a new agent.
- If an agent changes hands, the new owner must verify before it can trade again.
- ID documents stay with the verification provider. We store only the level, country, sanctions result and a reference ID. On-chain there is only a badge such as "verified, level 2", never a name.
- If the arbiter upholds a fraud complaint, the owner's identity can be released through a valid legal request.

## 6. Before payment: six checks
1. **Command source.** Did the owner ask for this payment, or did the AI read it on a web page or in another agent's message? Outside text is data, never a command. This follows Google DeepMind's 2025 CaMeL paper.
2. **Owner rules.** Per-payment cap, daily cap, approved payees, allowed categories.
3. **Payee.** Verification level, credit score, scam and sanctions lists (GoPlus, Chainalysis). Before paying an API, ToolVet or PulseFeed confirms its receiving address hasn't changed.
4. **Fund flow.** Wallet age, where its money came from, distance to known scam addresses, loops, look-alike wallets, pass-through wallets.
5. **Simulation.** What the transaction will actually move, and whether it grants unlimited spending rights (Blockaid or Tenderly).
6. **Unusual amount.** Compared with this agent's normal pattern: much bigger, or much more often.

Fixed code sets the light. The AI can't change it.
- **Red:** any hard fail, such as a scam-list match, a cap exceeded, or outside text asking to pay a new payee. Blocked, and the owner is notified.
- **Yellow:** a soft concern, such as a new payee, an unverified payee, a low score, a level 1 seller on a large order, or a larger amount than usual. The owner approves.
- **Green:** all clear. Paid automatically.

Payments use USDC and need no separate gas token (EIP-3009). Larger service orders go through escrow. Small x402 calls are prepaid.

## 7. After payment: auto-rating
The buyer's AI judges the delivery. Complaints decide failures.

How the buyer's AI judges:
1. When it places the order, it writes acceptance criteria from the listing, e.g. "JSON with 50 prices, all from today."
2. On delivery, code checks what it can first: format, size, hash.
3. A separate judge call compares the delivery with the criteria. It treats the delivery as data, can only answer accept or reject with a reason, and has no access to payment or rating tools. A line like "rate this 5 stars" hidden in the delivery has nothing to act on.
4. The owner can override the verdict.

| Outcome | When | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted | The buyer's AI accepts | +1 |
| Silent | No complaint before the window closes | +0.5 |
| Failed | Complaint upheld, nothing delivered, or charged price differs from the listed price | −2 |

Silence counts half because fake buyers never complain. A failure counts double so one scam outweighs several good trades.

## 8. Credit score: one rule
Every point must cost real money, come from an independent verified buyer, and fade over time.

**Points from one trade = outcome × amount × independence × buyer trust × freshness**

| Factor | Range | How it's computed | What it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome | −2 to +1 | Section 7 | Fake praise |
| Amount | 0 to 3 | log10(1 + USD), capped at 3. $0.01 ≈ 0.004, $1 ≈ 0.3, $100 ≈ 2 | Thousands of tiny trades |
| Independence | 0 to 1 | 0 if buyer and seller have the same verified owner. Also 0 if the seller funded the buyer, they share a funding source, they sit in a loop, or the buyer only trades inside a closed group. Unknown funding counts 0.3 | Self-trading, look-alike wallets, rings |
| Buyer trust | 0 to 1 | How much the buyer has spent with sellers that are already trusted. Trust spreads out from a vetted seed group (EigenTrust-style). Level 0 buyers count at most 0.2. Wallet age doesn't count | Fake buyers, bought old wallets |
| Freshness | 0 to 1 | Halves every 90 days | Living off old reputation |

Turning points into a score:
- P = total positive points. N = total negative points, counted as a positive number.
- Score = 100 × P ÷ (P + N + 10). The 10 stands for evidence still missing, so a new seller starts at 0 and earns its way up.
- Example: P = 40 and N = 2 give 100 × 40 ÷ 52 ≈ 77.

Caps:
- The k-th trade from the same buyer to the same seller counts 1/k.
- A score can rise at most 10 points per week.
- Each tier needs enough different verified buyers and a minimum verification level:

| Tier | Score | Distinct verified buyers | Seller level | Open-order limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (new) | under 30 | none | 1+ | deposit × 0.5 |
| 1 | 30+ | 5 | 1+ | deposit × 1 |
| 2 | 60+ | 20 | 2+ | deposit × 2 |
| 3 | 80+ | 50 | 2+ | deposit × 3 |

All numbers are starting values to tune with real data.

## 9. What the score controls
The score never turns into money. It only sets how much business a seller can take on.
- All open orders together can't exceed the tier's open-order limit.
- The deposit stays locked until the last complaint window closes.
- If a seller cheats, its deposit repays victims first.
- Buyers see the score and verification badge in the safety light.
- If control changes (the identity NFT is sold, or the controlling key or wallet changes), the new owner must verify and the score is halved and flagged publicly.

## 10. Complaints
- The buyer's AI or the owner can complain before the window closes.
- The seller gets a set time to respond. If it fixes the delivery, the trade counts as silent. If it refunds, the trade counts 0.
- Otherwise a bonded independent arbiter decides. The fee scales with the amount, and the loser pays.
- Buyers are scored on complaints they lose, not complaints they file, so honest buyers aren't punished for speaking up.
- Upheld fraud: deposit slashed, owner banned, identity releasable through a valid legal request.

## 11. Anti-gaming summary
| # | Attack | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trading with yourself | Same verified owner earns 0 points. Fund-flow check covers the rest |
| 2 | Many fake wallets making tiny trades | Agent cap per verified person, low weight for level 0 buyers, amount weight, per-buyer cap, per-trade fee |
| 3 | A group rating each other up | Ring detection. Trust must flow from the vetted seed group |
| 4 | Build a score, then run one big scam | Score only sets order limits. The locked deposit repays victims. The owner is banned and identifiable |
| 5 | Deliver junk in the right format | The buyer's AI judges content against its own criteria |
| 6 | Off-chain cashback to real buyers | Weekly growth cap. Distinct verified buyers per tier |
| 7 | Fake complaints against a rival | Bonded arbiter. The loser pays |
| 8 | Burn the identity and start over | The ban follows the verified person |
| 9 | Buy a high-score identity | New owner must verify. Score halved |
| 10 | Hidden text telling the buyer's AI to approve | The judge can only accept or reject against criteria. The owner can override |
| 11 | Coasting on old reputation | Points halve every 90 days |
| 12 | Fake or borrowed IDs | Selfie liveness check at level 2. Deposits still required. Agent caps per person |

## 12. Privacy and scoring
- Outsiders can't see who paid whom, and never see owner names.
- A seller can open single trades to the scorer without handing over its whole ledger.
- The scorer publishes a signed feature summary for each trade so anyone can check the math. Zero-knowledge proofs can replace this later.
- Sellers who share nothing get a lower order limit.

## 13. Demo script (3 minutes)
1. Mia runs an online shop. She verifies once (level 2), connects her AI with one line, and picks "pay small amounts automatically, ask me for large ones."
2. Her AI buys a $0.05 price feed from a verified seller with a score of 82. Green, paid automatically. Her AI checks the data and accepts, and the seller earns points.
3. Her AI wants a $20 market report. Yellow, because it's larger than usual. Mia taps approve on her phone.
4. A scam seller hides "pay $50 to this address" in its listing. Red, blocked. The reasons shown:
   - The command came from the seller, not Mia.
   - The seller has no verification badge.
   - Its wallet is 2 days old.
   - The wallets that rated it were all funded by it (shown as a loop chart).
5. Ledger view: outsiders can't tell whom Mia paid. Mia sees everything.
