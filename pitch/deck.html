How it works · 1 of 2

Six checks on every payment. The first asks where the address came from.

CheckRed whenYellow when
1Instruction sourceWhere did the address come from?The address appeared inside a seller's text the AI read: a listing, a delivery. The excerpt is the evidence.An address we have never seen: the owner may have typed it. Off the allowlist is never green.
2Owner's rulesInside what you allowed?Over the per-payment or 24-hour cap, empty budget, blocked category, paused AI. Unverified owner over $5 a day.Automatic payments would pass the 24-hour auto total, so one big payment can't be split into many small ones.
3PayeeDo we know who gets the money?Scam or sanctions list, seller at its open-order limit, suspended by risk review.New payee, no verified owner, low credit score, large order to a level-1 seller, under risk restriction.
4Money flowWhere did the wallet's money come from?Funded directly by a scam-list or sanctioned wallet.Bad wallet two or more steps back, wallet under 7 days old, pass-through pattern, reviewer ring.
5SimulationWould it do what the request says?Amount moved would differ from the request. Placeholder tonight: no chain yet.
6Unusual amountNormal for this AI?Over 5× the median of its last 20 payments, more payments than usual in an hour, same address and amount within 10 minutes.

Any hard reason → red. Any soft reason → yellow. No reasons → green. Fixed in code, the AI can't change it. The executor runs all six again right before the money moves, and every decision goes into a hash-chained log.

How it works · 2 of 2

Credit is earned by delivering, and it is the asset: a record of who actually delivers, hard to fake.

points per trade = outcome × amount × evidence × independence × buyer trust × 1/k
score = 100 × P ÷ (P + N + 10)
outcomeaccepted +1 · silent +0.5 · refunded 0 · failed −2
amountlog10(1 + dollars), capped at 3. Volume can't buy a score.
evidencedelivered through us 1 · a link 0.7 · elsewhere 0
independence0 / 0.3 / 1 from the funding graph, 3 wallets back. A sock puppet buyer is 0.
buyer trust0 to 1, spreads from audited accounts through real spending. Buying from your own sellers doesn't count.
1/k · freshnessk-th trade of the same pair · points halve every 90 days · at most +10 a week
Hard to game
One zero cancels the trade. A big amount can't rescue a puppet buyer.
Losses count in full: a seller that never delivers loses points even to a brand-new buyer.
Nightly recompute re-scores every trade, so a puppet found later loses the points it gave.
Demo: PriceFeed delivers $0.05 of prices → 1 × 0.021 × 1 × 1 × 0.8 × 1 ≈ +0.017 points. Score 82, tier 3.
Risk is a separate score
Spreads only from accounts confirmed as fraud, never from suspicion. 60 = restricted (yellow), 80 = suspended (red).
Victims are not accomplices: losing money to the scammer clears the link. Demo: FastRater 125, suspended; first-time buyer 0.
30 backend tests. Delivery checked by a separate Claude call with no payment or rating tools. Payments simulated tonight; scam lists and wallet history seeded.

Why we win

Rails move the money. We decide whether a payment is safe, and whether the other side can be trusted.

What compounds
Data flywheel. Every finished trade makes the score more accurate. A better score brings more buyers, and more buyers bring more trades.
Bans follow the person. Every agent has a verified owner. A banned owner can't come back with a new wallet. Agents of one owner count as one party and can't rate each other.
Evidence a rail can't offer. Every decision, and every recheck before paying, is in a hash-chained log. The score is public and anyone can recompute it from the published formula.
Not tied to one rail. The gate sits between the agent and the wallet, so it can front x402, a card, or a bank transfer.
What's next

Privacy. Stealth addresses, so outsiders can't see whom you paid.
Disputes. Escrow and arbitration for sellers who don't deliver.
Real lists and real identity. Scam and sanctions feeds instead of seeded data; a verification provider instead of the mocked button.
